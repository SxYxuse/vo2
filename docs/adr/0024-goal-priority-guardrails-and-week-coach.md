# Goal priority, load guardrails, and a week coach

The coach's layout of the rest of the week and the same easing for every B goal are superseded by the week plan in [0026](0026-the-week-is-planned-upfront-from-a-plan-built-on-the-season.md). Goal priority is superseded by [0052](0052-a-goal-is-a-main-goal-or-a-supporting-race-with-an-effort.md): a goal is a main goal or a supporting race with an effort.

Each goal has a priority. **A** goals are what the season is for: the plan builds, peaks, and fully tapers for the next A event, with a recovery week after it. **B** goals get a few lighter days in their week and no recovery week. **C** goals are trained through, like a hard session. With no A goal ahead, the next B goal leads; with neither, the plan holds fitness. Priority replaces the 4-week cluster rule and the main-goal pick.

The week target has three guardrails on top of the phase's ramp. It never asks for more than 10 % above the average of the last four weeks' load. Every fourth week before the peak is a lighter week at 70 % of the usual load, with one key session. And the two weeks after an A goal never ramp, however close the next one is. When the phase asks for more than a guardrail allows, the target says which one held it back.

Within the week, the long session is the last key session dropped: when key sessions would take more than 60 % of the target, intervals go first and the long one is shortened rather than removed. Toward a goal it leads the week in base and comes second from build on. It only counts when done on its own sport. Easy sessions are at least 45 minutes on a bike and 30 minutes on foot, so fewer days left means fewer, not shorter, sessions. Running time grows at most 15 % above the athlete's recent weekly running. The day before a goal is an opener, and the goal's own day shows the race, not a session.

VO2 now places the rest of the week on days, in two layers. The algorithm lays out a default: key sessions spaced apart (the long one on the day with the most time), easy time over the other days, and the rest day kept. The athlete can tell the coach about any day, either with a quick choice (can't train, short on time, indoor only, take it easy) or in their own words ("storm all afternoon, maybe 40 minutes on the trainer"). A language model reads those words into the same constraints, rearranges the default with the athlete's history and goal in mind, and writes a short note. Every model answer is checked against the algorithm's rules: known key sessions only, each once, no hard days back to back, the athlete's constraints honoured, and no more than the time left. An answer that fails any rule is dropped for the algorithm's layout with the parsed constraints. The plan is stored for the week and re-planned when a sync changes the week's activities or the athlete adds a note.

The ramp, cluster, and fixed-share rules each held when tested alone, but together they let a perform goal eight months out push a 60-fitness athlete toward 30 hours a week, then warned about the jump the plan itself set. Load guardrails bound the plan by what the athlete has actually done, which also makes a missing hours setting harmless. Priority is how athletes already describe races, and it lets a tune-up six weeks before a marathon stay a tune-up. Day placement was avoided in [0016](0016-week-target-replaces-the-week-bucket.md) because a fixed schedule broke whenever life got in the way. Letting the athlete say what got in the way, and re-planning from synced activities, keeps what was right about that while answering "what do I do on Saturday?". The model never sets load or invents sessions, so the plan stays testable, works without an API key, and keeps working offline from what was stored.

This supersedes the cluster and main-goal rules in [0023](0023-season-goals-drive-the-week-target.md) and the rule in [0016](0016-week-target-replaces-the-week-bucket.md) that VO2 does not pin sessions to days.

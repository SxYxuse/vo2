# Hard run steps name one exact pace

A coach plan's run step at marathon, threshold, interval, or repetition pace now names one pace in seconds per km (`secPerKm`) next to its pace type, and the athlete sees "3:58/km" instead of the range "3:52–4:03/km". The coach picks it: inside the pace type's range, or at the predicted race pace of an upcoming run goal, which VO2 now gives it (`racePaces`). The plan check refuses a pace outside both, within 2 seconds for rounding, and the step's time and distance are worked out from it.

A range is the right model of a pace type, but not something to run: on the watch the athlete holds one number, and a 10-second band left them to guess where in it the session wanted them. The coaching knowledge says where to pick: the slow end in base, the fast end in peak, longer repeats slower, beginners slower.

The coach picks rather than VO2, because the spot depends on the session: a 2 km threshold repeat sits slower than a 1 km one, which a rule by phase alone wouldn't see. VO2 still owns the paces: the coach can't step outside the range the athlete's fitness gives.

Race pace was written as an effort ("10 km effort, between T and I"). It now targets the goal's predicted race pace from the athlete's VDOT, the same prediction the season shows, so the session rehearses the pace the athlete is expected to race. A distance that is no goal stays an effort.

Easy steps, warm-ups, cool-downs, and jogs keep the easy range or an effort: easy is run by feel or heart rate (ADR 0042), and an exact number would pull the athlete to the watch. Plans written before keep their ranges until they are written again.

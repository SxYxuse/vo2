# The long run is at most twice the average of the other runs

Supersedes, in part, [0040](0040-the-coach-plan-is-written-when-the-app-asks-and-short-weeks-allow-a-bigger-long-run-share.md): the long run's share of the week.

The coach plan's longest run of a week is at most twice the average of the week's other runs, and never held under 30 %. As a share of the week that is 2 / (runs + 1): 67 % with two runs, 50 % with three, 40 % with four, 33 % with five, and 30 % from six. The runs are counted as ADR 0040 counts them, from the athlete's availability and the days left in the week. The 10 % rule on the longest run of the last 30 days and the 2 h 30 cap still hold, and the long ride's share is unchanged.

Daniels' 30 % was written for athletes running most days. ADR 0040 raised it to 40 % and 50 % for three and two runs, but that still kept a three-run athlete training for a half or full marathon near 14 km in a 36 km week, and left a two-run week no long run at all, since equal runs are already half of it. Twice the average reads the same at every run count, and meets Daniels' 30 % at six runs.

Runna, which many athletes come from, lets the long run take 50–66 % of a three-run week and grows it 20–33 % at a step in its sample marathon plans, and in 2026 faced press reports of injuries among its users. We take a bigger share without its growth: how fast the long run grows stays the 10 % rule's job, the one limit with cohort evidence behind it (a run longer than the recent longest predicts injury), so a bigger share lets a long run reach its length, not get there sooner.

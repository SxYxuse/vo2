# Season goals drive the week target

The cluster and main-goal rules are superseded by goal priority in [0024](0024-goal-priority-guardrails-and-week-coach.md). Fixed hours are superseded by the training plan in [0026](0026-the-week-is-planned-upfront-from-a-plan-built-on-the-season.md).

The athlete plans a season as a list of goals: races or events, each with a name, sport type, date, distance, elevation gain, and an objective (finish, finish well, or perform). Any sport type can be a goal. Build pace and hours intent go away; the week target is set by the goals instead. Fixed hours stay, as the time the athlete has each week, a cap rather than a lever for progression.

Goals are worked toward in date order. The next goal sets the phase from the weeks left to it: base more than about 12 weeks out, then build, peak, taper, and recovery after the date. Its objective sets how fast fitness rises and how much of the week is key sessions. Its distance and elevation gain shape the long session and the climbing. The time split between sports follows the sport types of all upcoming goals during base and leans on the next goal's sport from build on. With no goal ahead, the plan holds fitness.

The gap to the following goal decides what fits after recovery: a full rebuild from 8 weeks, or holding fitness and peaking again from 4 to 8 weeks. Goals less than 4 weeks apart form a cluster, because their tapers and recoveries would overlap. The athlete picks the main goal of a cluster, the one the plan peaks and fully tapers for; the others get a short taper and a few easy days after. Until the athlete picks, the longest goal by expected time is the main one. Goals on the same date are one event, such as a triathlon entered as a swim, a ride, and a run, and are all peaked for together. The phase and cluster rules are worked out from the goal list each time the page loads; only the goals and the athlete's main-goal choice are stored.

Build pace and hours intent asked the athlete how hard to push without saying what for. A race six months away calls for unhurried base work; the same push three weeks out is a peak. The goal carries that context, so the athlete describes the event and VO2 sets the urgency. Fitness, fatigue, and form become guardrails and a projection of form on race day, not the thing the plan steers by. Ordering by date keeps the season simple; priority is asked only where two goals compete for the same taper.

This supersedes the build pace and hours intent in [0016](0016-week-target-replaces-the-week-bucket.md). The week target, week mix, key and easy sessions, today's choice, and today's pick stay as 0016 describes them.

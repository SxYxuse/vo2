# The coach plan is written when the app asks, and short weeks allow a bigger long run share

Supersedes, in part, [0037](0037-the-coach-plan-is-written-by-the-coach-inside-a-budget-vo2-sets.md): the long run's 30 % share for weeks of two or three runs.

Choices made while building the coach plan (ADR 0037) that it did not settle, or that changed once the rules met real weeks.

**When writing starts.** VO2 has no scheduler, so the plan is written when the app loads it: opening This week or Settings → Training under the coach plan checks whether writing is due and, if so, starts it on the server and returns at once. The app checks back every few seconds while it writes. Writing is due for the first plan, when availability, goals, or paces change, when a key session was missed, when the athlete switches back to the coach plan, and when a new week is due (the current week and the three after it are kept written). A goal passing its day changes nothing. A key session counts as missed only once a sync ended on a later day, so a run not yet copied doesn't rewrite the plan. Each due writing is tried once per trigger (the change that set it off, the week being added, the VDOT in whole points): a failed one waits for the athlete's "Try again", so a plan the coach can't get right never spends their credit on every load. Paces drift a little every day (ADR 0039), so they count as changed only once the VDOT moved a full point, about 2 % in pace. Each model call stops after two and a half minutes, and writing marked as running for more than ten minutes is read as stopped with the server; only the writing that holds the current claim saves its result.

**The long run's share.** This supersedes ADR 0037's 30 % for weeks of two or three runs. ADR 0037 caps the long run at 30 % of the week, but with two or three runs even equal runs are half or a third of it, so a week of two runs allows 50 % and a week of three 40 %. The week's longest run is checked, whatever the coach calls it. The run count comes from the athlete's availability, not from how many runs the coach wrote: their runs per week, fewer when the days being written hold fewer of their days (a week rewritten from Thursday, a race on a running day), and no more than the budget holds at 3 km a run, so a small taper or recovery week isn't asked for 2 km runs. The 50 % and 40 % wait on the coach's review. The 2 h 30 cap and the 10 % rule on the longest run still hold.

**A floor under the budget.** A week written in full, other than a taper or race week, reaches at least 90 % of its budget, or of what the athlete's days allow when their time or the longest-run rule holds the week lower. The coaching knowledge asks for 95–100 %; the gap leaves room for rounding.

A scheduler would write a new week on Monday whether the athlete opens VO2 or not, but would spend their credit on weeks nobody looks at, and needs a worker VO2 doesn't run. Holding every week to 30 % would make the coach plan impossible for athletes who run two or three times a week, which the availability allows.

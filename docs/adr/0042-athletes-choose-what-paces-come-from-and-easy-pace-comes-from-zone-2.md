# Athletes choose what paces come from, and easy pace comes from zone 2

Supersedes, in part, [0039](0039-training-paces-come-from-the-best-recent-run-held-near-the-next-best.md): which best effort the estimate reads, and the race result as the only override.

A half-Cooper test (6 minutes all out, about 1.7 km) set the VDOT through its mile best effort, and the model over-reads 10 km and longer from efforts that short. Settings → Paces now lets the athlete say what the paces come from, and reads the easy pace from heart rate.

**Paces from.** Automatic (the estimate below), one of the athlete's best efforts, or a race result entered by hand. The best efforts to pick from are the fastest at 1 mile, 5 km, 10 km, half and marathon in road runs, the recent one (last 16 weeks) and the all-time one, each with its time, date and age. A picked effort sets the paces as it is, without the age discount, until the athlete changes it; past 16 weeks the page says so and offers the recent best at the same distance, but never drops it. The race result keeps ADR 0039's rule: it stops setting the paces at 16 weeks and Automatic takes over. Athletes from before the choice keep their race result, if any. The pick and the switch below are stored per athlete in `athlete_metrics.pace_settings`, which is removed with the athlete.

**A better Automatic.** The estimate reads the best-effort distance closest to the A goal's (the next running A goal that can be predicted; 10 km without one), by ratio, among distances that at least two runs of the last 16 weeks hold. Efforts of 3 km or more (the 2-mile best effort and up) are preferred: shorter ones count only when no such distance has two runs. At that distance ADR 0039 still holds: each run counts once, worth 1 % less per week of age, and the best is held within 5 % of the next best. Comparing efforts of one distance makes the cap meaningful, since a mile and a half marathon read differently even on the same day. Predicted race times read the same VDOT.

**Easy pace from zone 2.** The default: the pace the athlete holds in running heart-rate zone 2, cut by their zone method (ADR 0021), on road runs of the last 8 weeks. A sample counts when the run has been moving for 5 minutes (heart rate lags and climbs in a warm-up), Strava's smoothed grade is within ±2 % (flat), the smoothed speed is above 1.5 m/s (not a walk break), and no gap over 30 s precedes it. The easy range is the 25th to 75th percentile, by time, of those samples' pace: the middle half of the athlete's zone 2 running. It needs 60 minutes of such running; with less, or without running zones, the easy pace is the VDOT's, with a note saying why. "Easy pace from" switches between Heart rate (zone 2) and Fitness estimate (VDOT); the other paces always come from the VDOT. Each run's flat running is cached by heart rate and pace on its stream row, so a zone change reads the cache rather than every stream.

**Rewrites.** A changed VDOT, whatever its source, rewrites the coach plan only under ADR 0040's full point. An easy pace from heart rate moves on its own, so the plan is also rewritten when the middle of the easy pace moved 10 s/km or more since the plan was written, when the easy pace came from heart rate then or now. An easy pace from the VDOT moves with it and is left to the VDOT rule. 10 s/km is about what a VDOT point does to easy pace. Plans written before this have no easy pace kept and are not rewritten for it.

Heart rate tells easy pace apart from fitness better than a share of VDOT: two athletes of the same VDOT can hold different paces aerobically, and easy days are where VO2's sessions spend most of their time. The trade-offs: heat, fatigue and caffeine move zone 2 pace, which the 8-week window and the middle half only smooth; an athlete whose zones are wrong gets a wrong easy pace, which is why the source is shown; and a picked effort can go on overstating fitness until the athlete acts on the reminder.

# A week target replaces the bucket of planned sessions

Build pace and hours intent superseded by [0023](0023-season-goals-drive-the-week-target.md). Not pinning sessions to days superseded by the day plan in [0024](0024-goal-priority-guardrails-and-week-coach.md). The week mix, fixed hours, and today's choice superseded by the week plan in [0026](0026-the-week-is-planned-upfront-from-a-plan-built-on-the-season.md).

VO2 no longer stores planned sessions or matches activities to them. The training plan sets a week target in hours (with training load as the second number), derived from fitness and build pace unless the athlete fixes the hours. The athlete sets a week mix (sessions per week and the share that are key sessions); VO2 generates the key sessions (intervals, time in zone, or a long Z2) from build pace and fitness, and spreads the rest of the target over easy sessions. The Week target page shows time left, ticks key sessions off, picks one session for the time the athlete has today, and projects fitness and form if they finish the week. Everything is worked out from copied activities each time the page loads.

The bucket was mostly filler ("Easy ride 1h 15"), matching by duration failed whenever a ride ran long, and the session counts and double days in Settings existed only to fill that list. Time left was the one part athletes used. A target plus one recommendation keeps that, uses the fitness model and heart-rate zones VO2 already has, and drops the stored state that had to be rebuilt after every sync.

The auto target is read from fitness at the start of Monday, so it does not move during the week. Every sport type counts toward the hours. A key session is done when one activity spends 80 % of its work time at or above its zone, from the heart-rate stream, or from an estimate of hard time when there is no stream; one activity ticks off one session, hardest first. Key sessions pause when form is below −30. A fixed hard-time share per build pace was tried first, then hand-built key sessions; the first hid the athlete's intent behind a binary switch, the second made them design workouts. The athlete now picks only counts and a split, and VO2 does the building. The cost is that VO2 no longer lists several sessions ahead; it still does not pin anything to a day.

The athlete has the last word on today. They can say which sport (or a run then a ride) and whether they feel like going easy or hard; VO2 builds today's pick around that, shortens a key session to fit, and adds an extra one if the mix has none left. It does not refuse: going against its own pick shows a warning with the cost and a one-tap way back to VO2's pick. The week then re-plans only from synced activities, suggested or not, and drops key sessions that no longer fit the days left instead of stacking them.

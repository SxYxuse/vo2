# The week is planned upfront from a plan built on the season

The athlete builds a plan once, in four steps: their season, where they are now, when they can train, and a preview. Every answer starts from their Strava history and the goals already on the Season page; the builder never creates goals. The plan stores the week's shape (rest days, a long session day, how many days can take two sessions, and which sports to plan) and a copy of the goals it was built on. Settings → Training edits the same shape and can rebuild the plan. The week mix, the hours cap, and the single preferred rest day go away.

Each training week is laid out Monday to Sunday the first time it is opened, with one or more sessions on each training day and nothing on rest days; days of that week already gone by then are left unplanned and show only what was done. Days already behind stay as they were planned, so the week reads as planned, done, missed, or extra. The rest of the week is planned again only when something changes it: a key session missed or done on another day, a note to the coach, a change to the week's shape, or adapting to the season. Missed easy sessions are not made up. The language model no longer lays out days: it reads the athlete's words into the same day constraints (can't train, short on time, indoor only, take it easy) and writes a short note, and the algorithm does the layout.

The number of key sessions follows the objective of the goal the plan leads to: one for finish, two for finish well, three for perform, within the phase's own limit and never more than half the training days, so hard days never sit back to back. With a long session day to fill, the long session leads in base and comes second otherwise, with or without a goal. Every goal shapes the days around it by its priority and objective. A goals keep their full build, peak, taper, and recovery week. A B goal gets one easy day before it to finish, two lighter days, an opener, and an easy day after to finish well, and three lighter days, an opener, and two easy days after to perform, in a week at 85 % with one key session. A C goal is trained through: an easy day before it to finish well, and easy days before and after to perform. A B or C race counts as the week's long session when it is on the long session's sport, and replaces an easy session otherwise.

The plan reads its phases from its own copy of the goals, not from the Season page. After any change to the goals, VO2 compares the two week by week through the end of the current block, the leading goal and its recovery week. When no week changes, such as a new A goal after the current one, the copy updates at once and a note says when the plan picks it up. When a week changes, the athlete sees which weeks change and chooses to adapt the plan or keep it; a kept plan shows a reminder on This week until it is adapted or the change no longer matters. With no goal ahead, the plan holds fitness on the athlete's usual days and asks for a goal.

Without history, the week starts from 45 minutes per training day. An athlete coming back from a break can set where they start; the plan then grows from there by at most 10 % a week until it meets what fitness asks for.

The week mix asked athletes to design a week in counts and shares they could not judge, then filled it with a session for whatever time they had today. Athletes asked instead what to do on each day, the way a coach or Runna lays out a week. A whole week planned from the start answers that, keeps the rules that held (load guardrails, key sessions spaced apart, races and openers), and turns the athlete's choices into what they actually know: which days they can train and when they have the most time. Keeping a copy of the goals lets a season change be shown before it reshapes the week, and lets a far-off race wait until it matters.

This supersedes the week mix, fixed hours, and today's choice in [0016](0016-week-target-replaces-the-week-bucket.md), the fixed hours in [0023](0023-season-goals-drive-the-week-target.md), and in [0024](0024-goal-priority-guardrails-and-week-coach.md) the coach's layout of the rest of the week and the same handling of every B goal. The phases, guardrails, and key session types stay as those ADRs describe them.

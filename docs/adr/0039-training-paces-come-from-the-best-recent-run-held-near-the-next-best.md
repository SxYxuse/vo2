# Training paces come from the best recent run, held near the next best

Training paces (ADR 0037) are read from a VDOT, with Daniels and Gilbert's equations. Without a race result the athlete set, VO2 estimates it from best efforts inside road runs (sport type Run) of the last eight weeks. Each run counts once, by its best effort of a mile or more. The best run sets the VDOT, but at most 5 % above the next-best run, and with fewer than two such runs there are no paces. Hidden sport types count, since hiding changes views, not fitness (ADR 0018). A race result the athlete sets (distance, time, date) wins over the estimate until they clear it, as set anchors do (ADR 0021). Predicted race times are the same model solved for time, from the mile to the marathon, and shown on Season for road-run goals only.

Best efforts inside training runs are a floor, not a race, so taking the best one rather than a median keeps the estimate from erring further slow. Holding it near the next-best run means one GPS slip or unusual day can lift every pace by at most 5 %, which is what "paces change gradually" asks for without storing a history. Efforts under a mile are left out because the model is fitted to races of about 3.5 minutes and up and over-reads sprints. Eight weeks is the window research notes suggest for a result to still describe fitness. Trail, treadmill, and virtual runs are left out for the reasons in ADR 0033, and a trail goal's time depends on its terrain more than on pace.

The trade-off is that the estimate drops in one step when the best run leaves the window, and an athlete who only runs easy gets slow paces until they set a race result.

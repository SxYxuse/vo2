# The coach plan plans runs and rides toward the season, and the week planner is removed

Supersedes [0026](0026-the-week-is-planned-upfront-from-a-plan-built-on-the-season.md) (the week planner, the plan builder, the training plan, and season changes), the week planner and its choice in [0037](0037-the-coach-plan-is-written-by-the-coach-inside-a-budget-vo2-sets.md), and the A running goal and runs-only plan of 0037. It extends [0046](0046-the-coach-chat-loads-running-knowledge-as-skills.md) with cycling and season skills.

**One planning style.** The athlete picks the coach plan or off. The week planner goes, with the plan builder, Settings → Training's week shape, the season-change prompt, the week plan's coach notes and their model choice, and the week planner's session page. A migration moves athletes on the week planner, and those who built its plan before the choice existed, to the coach plan, so they keep planning and skip onboarding; it drops the stored training plan and week plans. Old `/plan` links open Plan. The season's time zone now comes from the coach plan's availability.

**Runs and rides.** Availability is runs a week and rides a week, 0 to 6 each and at least two sessions in all; availability saved with runs only reads as no rides. Every session has a sport. The plan builds toward the next A goal that is a run or a ride with a distance, and toward the rest of the season: every upcoming run and ride goal in the weeks written is added as a race, and the coach sees every goal. An A goal whose sport has no sessions a week can't be written, and says so.

**Budgets per sport.** Runs keep their weekly kilometres (0037). Rides are budgeted in minutes: a ride's distance hangs on terrain, wind, and drafting, so cycling coaches plan by time or training stress, not kilometres. A riding week grows at most 10 % over the biggest of the last four weeks, or 30 minutes for a small week; its floor is 60 % of the usual week after a break, or 60 minutes a ride without history. Each week, only the sports of the event it builds toward (the next A goal, else the next B) grow with the phase; the other sport holds where it is. Lighter weeks, recovery, and the A goal's taper apply to both sports. A ride A goal tapers two weeks when it lasts five hours or more (85 %, 60 %, then 50 % in race week), one week otherwise (75 %, then 50 %), after Bosquet 2007 and cycling coaching sources.

**Ride rules, checked in code.** Ride steps are by time only and target a cycling zone (recovery to sprint, Coggan's levels as a share of FTP, with sweet spot) or an effort. No ride is more than 15 % longer than the longest ride of the 30 days before its week, or 90 minutes without one: rides carry no impact, so they may grow faster than runs' 10 %. The long ride is at most 6 hours and 40 % of the week's riding, 50 % with three rides, 60 % with two. Quality rides follow the runs' count by rides a week, and a week holds at most three quality sessions across runs and rides. Each step stores its watts from FTP and, up to threshold, its heart rate from cycling LTHR, so a ride reads in the athlete's numbers; an FTP that moved 5 % writes the plan again.

**The season on Plan.** Season's map goes: its bars were the hours a load model expected, which the coach plan's budgets don't keep, so it showed a second plan beside the real one. Plan shows the season instead, beside the week on desktop and below it on phones: every week through the last goal coloured by phase, the written weeks raised and goals marked by priority; the written weeks with each one's running and riding and how much is done, a tap showing that week; and the next goals ahead, of every sport, with their priority, distance, climbing, and what the plan does for each (builds to it, races it to finish well, trains through it, or doesn't plan its sport). Season keeps the goals and their editing. The coach's season tool keeps each week's phase.

**Knowledge.** The coach writes rides from `cycling/principles.md`, `cycling/zones.md`, and `cycling/sessions.md`, and a mixed season from `season.md`, researched from published sources. Plan writing loads only the files of the sports planned; the chat loads them as skills.

The week planner and the coach plan answered the same question twice, and athletes asked for the coach plan's precise sessions. Keeping both meant two planners, two session pages, and two sets of rules to maintain, while the week planner's notes and key sessions had already drifted from the coach plan's. Dropping it leaves athletes without an OpenRouter key with no plan; off still keeps Season, the calendar, fitness, and the coach chat. Holding the other sport, rather than splitting one budget across sports by share, keeps each budget grounded in what the athlete already does in that sport, and keeps the cross-training choices (which easy runs a ride may stand in for) with the coach, where the knowledge files guide them.

## Considered options

- **One budget in hours or training load, split across sports:** one number to grow, but a run's distance is what its injury rules read, and a split by share has no source to set it.
- **Keeping the week planner for athletes without a key:** a plan for everyone, at the cost of maintaining two planners that disagree.
- **Planning swims too:** triathletes would get every leg, but swim sessions need their own paces and rules; their swims still count toward load and the coach sees them.

# The coach plan is written by the coach inside a budget VO2 sets

Partly superseded by [0048](0048-the-coach-plans-sessions-have-no-day-or-order.md): the coach plan's sessions have no day, and a session is marked done by hand. Partly superseded by [0050](0050-the-coach-plan-plans-runs-and-rides-and-the-week-planner-is-removed.md): the week planner is removed, and the coach plan plans rides beside runs, toward an A run or ride.

The athlete picks a planning style in onboarding and in Settings → Training: off, the week planner (ADR 0026, unchanged), or the coach plan. Each style keeps its own setup, so switching back restores it. Off keeps Season and the calendar and plans nothing.

The coach plan starts with running. It needs an upcoming A running goal with a distance and the athlete's OpenRouter key, since writing the plan is a coach feature with its own model choice (ADR 0036). The athlete sets how many runs a week (two to six), the days they can run, the time they have each day (dropped in [0041](0041-coach-plan-availability-has-no-time-per-day.md)), and the long run day. The coach writes precise sessions for the next four weeks: steps by distance or time, each with a pace range or effort, and repeats. It writes one new week each Monday. Written weeks are written again from the first day affected when the athlete changes availability or goals, asks the coach, misses a key session, or their paces change. Weeks gone by stay as they were. Rides, swims, and other sports still count toward load and are given to the coach as context, but only runs are planned.

VO2 sets the budget and checks the result; the coach only writes sessions inside it. Weekly kilometres follow the season phase and never exceed 15 % above the biggest of the last four weeks, or a floor for athletes returning from a break or without history. Every plan the coach writes is checked in code: no run more than 10 % longer than the longest of the last 30 days, never two hard days in a row nor the long run after one, one quality session a week for two or three runs, two for four or five, at most three for six, a long run of at most 30 % of the week (more in weeks of two or three runs: see [0040](0040-the-coach-plan-is-written-when-the-app-asks-and-short-weeks-allow-a-bigger-long-run-share.md)) and 2 h 30, every fourth week lighter, a two-week taper before a marathon and one to two before shorter races, and no missed session made up by doubling. A plan that breaks a rule goes back to the coach with the reason, and is dropped after two retries; the previous plan stays until a new one passes. Plans are written on the server, so closing the app does not stop one.

Paces come from the athlete's current fitness, estimated from recent best efforts with Daniels' VDOT, and a race result the athlete sets wins over the estimate. They change only gradually, never on one outlier run. Goals have no target time: the coach shows the predicted race time instead. With the coach plan, goals hide their objective and are saved as perform.

The coaching knowledge the coach writes from (load and structure, paces, a library of sessions) is plain Markdown in the repository, researched from published sources and reviewed, and loaded whole into the prompt. A planned session counts as done when a run on its day covers at least 70 % of its distance; the athlete can link or unlink a run by hand, and that choice wins.

The week planner lays out time and zones from rules, which suits athletes who want the shape of a week; it can't say "6 × 300 m at 3:50 with 200 m jog", and athletes asked for that, the way Runna and Campus Coach write sessions. A model writes such sessions well but keeps rules only as long as it feels like it: published reviews of plans written by ChatGPT rated them poorly, and the studies that fixed this enforced limits in code. Keeping the budget and the rules in code, and the writing in the model, gives precise sessions without letting the model decide how much the athlete runs. The 15 % cap replaces the 10 % weekly rule, which no trial supports; comparing with the biggest recent week, not last week, lets a lighter week pass without holding the next one back. Four weeks ahead gives the athlete something to plan their life around without paying for, and then throwing away, weeks that a missed run or a new goal would change.

## Considered options

- **Writing every week to race day at once:** what Runna shows, but costly on the athlete's key, stale after the first missed week, and too long a reply to check reliably.
- **Letting the coach set the load too:** simpler, but every limit becomes a request the model may ignore.
- **Replacing the week planner:** one planner to maintain, but it would remove a working feature that suits athletes who don't want a model involved or have no key.
- **Loading coaching knowledge on demand as skills:** worth it once triathlon brings three sports; for running alone the knowledge fits in the prompt.

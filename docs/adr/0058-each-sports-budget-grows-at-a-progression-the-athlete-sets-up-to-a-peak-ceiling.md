# Each sport's budget grows at a progression the athlete sets, up to a peak ceiling

Supersedes, in part, [0037](0037-the-coach-plan-is-written-by-the-coach-inside-a-budget-vo2-sets.md) (budgets that grow by phase without an upper bound) and [0050](0050-the-coach-plan-plans-runs-and-rides-and-the-week-planner-is-removed.md) (the other sport holding, and the season on Plan shown without volumes).

**Progression.** In Settings → Training, the athlete sets each plan sport's weekly growth with a slider from 0 to +10 % a week, marked Hold 0, Gentle 3, Standard 5, Bold 7, and Steep 10. The value is the base phase's growth; build grows 1.4 times as fast and peak 0.6 times, as the phase rates did (5, 7, 3 %). A sport the athlete hasn't set grows at Standard in the weeks that build toward an A event in that sport and holds otherwise, which is the behaviour before this ADR: with one A goal, its sport at Standard and the other at Hold. Either sport may now grow. When the next A goal's sport is on Hold, Plan says it won't build toward that goal; the setting never changes itself.

**Peak ceiling.** Growth stops at a ceiling, then holds; lighter weeks and the taper still apply under it. VO2 recommends `max(usual, min(raceMax, usual × 2, max(raceFloor, usual × G)))`, where the usual week is the average of the best four of the last 26 weeks, or the no-history floor (3 km a run, 60 minutes a ride) when there are none, and never under the level the plan starts from (the best three of the last four weeks), so a recent build is never cut. G is 1.5 for runs and 1.35 for rides. The race floor and max come from that sport's next A goal, else its next goal, so a B 10K before an A marathon doesn't cap the marathon's build: for runs 5K 25–60 km, 10K 30–70, half 40–90, marathon 55–120, ultra (45 km and more) 65–130; for rides max(4 h, 1.5 × the event's hours) to min(15 h, max(8 h, 3 × the event's hours)). Without a goal in the sport, the ceiling is `min(usual × G, usual × 2)`, never above 120 km or 15 h. An athlete already above the ceiling holds and is never cut. When the ceiling falls below the race floor, VO2 says so and doesn't raise it. The athlete may instead set their own peak per sport, higher or lower, in steps of 5 km or 30 minutes, up to 200 km or 25 h; above the recommendation, Settings and Plan show by how much, and the coach is told, to bring up only when relevant.

**What stays.** Every week-to-week rule holds whatever the progression or peak: 15 % (runs) and 10 % or 30 minutes (rides) over the biggest of the last four weeks, no session more than 10 % (runs) or 15 % (rides) longer than the longest of the last 30 days, the long session's share, lighter weeks, and tapers. A high peak is reached gradually, or not within a short season.

**The season on Plan.** VO2 computes budgets for every week through the last goal, not only the four written ones; weeks not yet written are a forecast recomputed from what the athlete does. Plan shows two charts on one timeline, running in kilometres and riding in hours, coloured by phase, written weeks outlined, goals as dots, the current week filling as it is done; a tap shows a week. They are hidden when planning is off or no A run or ride lies ahead.

**Rewrites.** Changing progression or peak rewrites the plan from next Monday only if a written week's sessions would fall outside the 90–105 % of its new budget that the plan check accepts (under 90 % counts only when the budget grew: a week the coach already held lower, around a race or by the long-session rules, stays); otherwise later weeks take the new budgets when written.

Projected over a whole season, the phase rates compounded without bound: 30 km a week became about 118 km over 28 weeks. That never showed while budgets were set four weeks at a time from recent history, but a season view made it visible. No study sets a peak volume. The published plans (Daniels, Pfitzinger, Higdon) size the peak from what the athlete already runs, about 1.2 to 2.5 times; Friel's cycling hours stay nearly flat across a season (the biggest week about 1.35 times the average); and the race only sets a floor, after Rasmussen 2013 (marathoners under 30 km a week were injured about twice as often). The weekly growth rates themselves have no evidence behind them (Buist 2008, Damsted 2018, Frandsen 2025); the single-session spike does, so it stays enforced while the growth rate becomes a preference. The ceiling is a recommendation, not a limit, because athletes who know their training should not be blocked from using the plan; the week-to-week rules carry the safety.

## Considered options

- **A peak target as the main control:** the number athletes know, but every target then needs a growth rate derived from it, and a target above what the caps allow reads as a promise the plan can't keep. It survives as the optional "my own peak".
- **Growth sliders per phase:** precise, but athletes don't think in base and build rates.
- **Three presets only:** simpler, but athletes asked for finer control; the slider keeps the names as marks.
- **A hard ceiling:** safer by default, but blocks experienced athletes from the plan.

# A time out backs the coach plan off and ramps it back up

Supersedes, in part, [0058](0058-each-sports-budget-grows-at-a-progression-the-athlete-sets-up-to-a-peak-ceiling.md): the floor of 60 % of the usual week no longer holds in the weeks after a time out.

The athlete marks a time out when they are sick or injured: a start date, an end date or none while it lasts, and the kind. The start date may be from four weeks before the current training week to four weeks ahead, and time outs never overlap. Both kinds act alike on every plan sport. The kind is passed to the coach; an injury that rules out only one sport is said through availability or the coach brief ([0065](0065-the-athlete-gives-the-coach-a-standing-brief.md)). Training load, fitness, fatigue, and form are computed as before: a day without activities already adds no load. The fitness chart and the calendar only show the time out.

**Backing off.** A week a time out overlaps keeps each sport's budget in proportion to its days outside the time out, so a week entirely inside one is written empty. Sessions have no day ([0048](0048-the-coach-plans-sessions-have-no-day-or-order.md)), so the cut is on the week's budget, not on days. While a time out is open, the coach plan writes nothing: the current week's open sessions are dropped, done ones stay, and Plan shows the time out with "I'm back". Ending it, setting or editing its dates, or deleting it rewrites the weeks ahead ([0040](0040-the-coach-plan-is-written-when-the-app-asks-and-short-weeks-allow-a-bigger-long-run-share.md)).

**Ramping up.** The first week back keeps a share of the budget that week would have had without the time out: 80 % after 1–6 days off, 60 % after 7–13, 40 % after 14 or more. Each later week adds 15 points until it is back at 100 %, and then the progression resumes. The 60 % floor does not apply during the ramp. A lighter week, taper, or supporting race in the ramp takes the smaller of its share and the ramp's, never both. The volume ramp is the only hard limit; the coach is told about the time out and advised to keep the first week back easy.

Without this, the floor that brings an athlete back near their usual week after an unexplained gap would put them straight back to 60 % or more of it after two weeks in bed. A gap in the history can't say whether the athlete was on holiday or ill; the athlete can. The shares follow common return-to-training advice (roughly half the time off to rebuild, sooner after a short illness), not a study; they are a cautious default.

## Considered options

- **Rewrite every day while a time out is open, as if the athlete were back tomorrow:** always has a plan, but spends the athlete's credit on weeks nobody follows.
- **Per-sport time outs for injuries:** fits an injured runner who can still ride, but availability and the coach brief already say that.
- **A hard rule against quality sessions in the first week back:** a new rule to check for one week; the shrunken budget already keeps the week small.
- **Lower fitness and fatigue during a time out:** they measure load absorbed, which is already zero without activities ([0033](0033-progress-is-read-from-best-efforts-and-same-heart-rate-output.md)).

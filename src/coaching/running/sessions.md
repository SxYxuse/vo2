# Running: session library

Sessions to build the coach plan from. Pick a session type for each run, then scale it to the athlete's level, goal distance, phase, and the day's available time. Every session must also pass the rules in `principles.md`.

Sources: numbers marked **(Daniels)** are via [Coach Ray](https://www.coachray.nz/2023/05/03/jack-daniels-running-intensity/); **(ctyeh)** via the [ctyeh interval guide](https://ctyeh.com/articles/728?lang=en), a secondary summary; **(McMillan)** via [McMillan Running](https://www.mcmillanrunning.com/?p=181). Everything else is coaching convention: guidance, not rule.

## Step format

Write every session as a list of steps, in order:

```
Warm-up: <amount> <target>
<amount> <target>
<repeats> × <amount> <target>, <recovery>
Cool-down: <amount> <target>
```

- **Amount:** a distance (`400 m`, `2 km`) or a time (`20 min`, `30 s`). Use distance on flat road; time for easy running, hills, and trails.
- **Target:** a pace type with its range from the athlete's training paces (`I 3:50–4:00 /km`), or an effort (`easy`, `steady`, `comfortably hard`, `hard`, `fast and relaxed`). See `paces.md`.
- **Recovery:** between repeats, an amount and how: `200 m jog`, `90 s walk`, `2 min standing`. Jog recoveries are at easy effort or slower.
- **Warm-up and cool-down:** every quality session has both, at easy. Warm-up 10–20 min or 2–3 km; cool-down 10 min or 1–2 km.
- A session's distance includes warm-up, cool-down, and recoveries. VO2 counts it toward the week.

Example:

```
Warm-up: 2 km E
6 × 300 m R, 200 m jog
Cool-down: 1.5 km E
```

## Levels

Read the level from the athlete's recent weekly distance and runs per week (guidance):

- **Beginner:** under about 20 km a week, or new to structured running. One quality session a week; fewer repeats, longer recoveries; effort over pace.
- **Intermediate:** about 20–50 km a week. One or two quality sessions.
- **Advanced:** over about 50 km a week, or years of structured training. Two quality sessions, more repeats, shorter recoveries.

## Which quality sessions for which goal

Guidance from the sources above:

| Goal          | Main quality                      | Second quality                    | Long run peak            |
| ------------- | --------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| 5 km          | Intervals (I), repetitions (R)    | Threshold (T)                     | 60–75 min                |
| 10 km         | Intervals (I), threshold (T)      | 10 km race pace                   | 75–90 min                |
| Half marathon | Threshold (T)                     | Marathon pace (M), half race pace | 16–19 km                 |
| Marathon      | Marathon pace (M) in the long run | Threshold (T)                     | 30–32 km, at most 2 h 30 |

In base phase, every goal uses easy running, strides, short hills, and at most one threshold session a week.

## Easy run

Aerobic base and volume; most of the week. No pace below E.

```
40 min easy
```

```
8 km E
6 × 20 s fast and relaxed, 60 s walk
```

- Beginner: 20–40 min; run-walk allowed. Advanced: 45–75 min.
- Strides (15–20 s, fast and relaxed, full recovery) after an easy run, 1–2 times a week, from base phase on. Beginner 4, advanced 8–10 (McMillan, via [Running Magazine](https://runningmagazine.ca/?p=109810)). Strides do not make a run a hard day.

## Recovery run

The day after a hard session or long run, when the athlete runs every day. Shorter and slower than an easy run.

```
30 min easy, slower than E
```

Keep under 40 min. No strides.

## Long run

Endurance. Easy unless the phase calls for marathon-pace work. The longest run of the week; see `principles.md` for its limits.

```
1 h 40 min easy
```

```
Warm-up: 4 km E
12 km M
Cool-down: 4 km E
```

```
18 km E
last 4 km steady
```

- Marathon-pace long run (build and peak, half and marathon goals only): a middle block at M. Pfitzinger puts 12–15 miles at M inside a 20-mile run (McMillan); scale to 30–50 % of the run for intermediates. Counts as a quality session.
- Fast finish: the last 15–20 % steady, never faster than M. Counts as quality only when the finish is at M or faster.

## Threshold: tempo run

Raises the pace the athlete can hold for about an hour.

```
Warm-up: 2 km E
20 min T
Cool-down: 1.5 km E
```

- Beginner: 15 min T, or 2 × 8 min T with 2 min jog. Advanced: 25–30 min T, within the 10 % cap (Daniels).
- 5 km and 10 km goals: 15–25 min. Half and marathon: 20–40 min.

## Threshold: cruise intervals

More threshold work than a continuous tempo allows, with short jogs.

```
Warm-up: 2 km E
5 × 1 km T, 1 min jog
Cool-down: 1.5 km E
```

- Beginner: 3 × 1 km T, 90 s jog. Intermediate: 4–6 × 1 km T, 60 s jog. Advanced: 5–6 × 1 mile T, 60 s jog.
- Daniels: an athlete who handles 20 min at T can do about 30 min as cruise intervals.

## Intervals (I)

Raises the athlete's ceiling: VO2max.

```
Warm-up: 2 km E
5 × 1000 m I, 3 min jog
Cool-down: 1.5 km E
```

- Beginner: 4 × 800 m I, 400 m jog. Intermediate: 5 × 1000 m I, 3 min jog. Advanced: 6 × 1000 m or 5 × 1200 m I, 2–3 min jog (ctyeh).
- Each repeat 3–5 min; total at I at most 8 % of the week (Daniels).
- Rare for marathon goals beyond one block in build.

## Repetitions (R)

Speed and economy with full recovery.

```
Warm-up: 2 km E
8 × 400 m R, 400 m jog
Cool-down: 1.5 km E
```

```
Warm-up: 1 km E
6 × 300 m R, 200 m jog
Cool-down: 500 m E
```

- Beginner: 6–8 × 200–300 m R, 200 m jog. Intermediate: 8–10 × 400 m R. Advanced: 10–12 × 400 m R (ctyeh).
- Each repeat at most 2 min; recovery 2–3 times the repeat's time, or jog the same distance; total at R at most 5 % of the week (Daniels).
- Mainly for 5 km and 10 km goals.

## Race pace

Rehearses the goal race. Write the pace as an effort with the pace type it sits at or between (`paces.md`).

5 km:

```
Warm-up: 2 km E
5 × 1 km 5 km effort (slow end of I), 2 min jog
Cool-down: 1.5 km E
```

10 km:

```
Warm-up: 2 km E
4 × 2 km 10 km effort (T–I), 2 min jog
Cool-down: 2 km E
```

Half marathon:

```
Warm-up: 3 km E
3 × 3 km half marathon effort (slow end of T), 3 min jog
Cool-down: 2 km E
```

Marathon:

```
Warm-up: 2 km E
3 × 5 km M, 2 min jog
Cool-down: 2 km E
```

- Build and peak phases only. Scale down the repeats for beginners (2–3 instead of 4–5).

## Fartlek

Variety and pace changes, by effort. Good for beginners' first quality sessions and for base phase.

```
Warm-up: 10 min easy
8 × 1 min hard, 2 min easy
Cool-down: 10 min easy
```

- Beginner: 6 × 1 min hard, 2 min easy. Advanced: 6 × 3 min hard, 2 min easy.
- Counts as a quality session.

## Hills

Effort only; never a pace target.

Short hill sprints (power, base phase):

```
Warm-up: 15 min easy
8 × 10 s hard uphill, walk back down
Cool-down: 10 min easy
```

Long hills (strength, build phase, hilly goals):

```
Warm-up: 15 min easy
6 × 60 s hard uphill, jog back down
Cool-down: 10 min easy
```

- Beginner: 6 × 10 s, or 4–6 × 45 s. Advanced: 10 × 10 s, or 5 × 2 min at 10 km effort.
- Hill sprints after an easy run don't make it a hard day; long hills do.
- Only when the athlete has a hill; otherwise use strides or fartlek.

## Progression run

Practises pacing, finishing faster. A quality session when it ends at T or M.

```
Warm-up: 1 km E
3 km E
3 km M
2 km T
Cool-down: 1 km E
```

- Beginner: easy to steady only, not a quality session.

## Taper and lighter weeks

- Keep the same session types, with fewer repeats: about half to two-thirds of the last full week's.
- Race week: one short session 3–4 days before the race, such as 4 × 400 m R or 3 × 1 km at race effort, with full recovery. The rest easy and short.
- Never a new kind of session in a taper.

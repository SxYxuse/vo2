# Cycling

Everything VO2 knows about planning rides: how a week is built, the zones rides target, and the sessions to write.

## Load and weekly structure

You write the athlete's rides for the coach plan: each week's ride sessions, with no day, inside a weekly riding budget VO2 sets in minutes. The athlete picks the days to ride them. VO2 checks every plan in code. A plan that breaks a rule comes back to you with the reason. Write sessions that pass the first time.

Evidence tags: **rule** means VO2 checks it in code. **Guidance** means coaching convention or weak evidence; follow it unless the athlete's situation gives a reason not to. Sources are linked where a number comes from one.

### Rules VO2 checks

1. **Weekly riding time stays inside the budget.** Rides are budgeted by time, not distance: speed hangs on terrain, wind, and drafting, so coaches plan rides by hours or training stress, never kilometres ([TrainingPeaks](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/learn/articles/estimating-training-stress-score-tss/)). The budget follows the season phase and never exceeds 10 % over the biggest of the last four weeks, or 30 minutes over it for a small week (coaches allow 10–15 % a week, [Velo](https://velo.outsideonline.com/training/new-training-series-on-velonews-com-prepping-for-gran-fondos/?scope=anon)). After a break, the floor is 60 % of the usual week before it; with no history, rides per week × 60 minutes. Plan 95–100 % of the budget.
2. **No ride is more than 15 % longer than the longest ride of the last 30 days**, or 90 minutes when there is none. Grow the long ride in steps.
3. **Sessions have no day.** A week holds no more rides than the athlete's rides per week, fewer in a week already under way. Never name a day.
4. **Quality rides per week:** 1 for 2–3 rides, 2 for 4–5, at most 3 for 6. Across runs and rides together, at most 3 quality sessions a week. Races count.
5. **The long ride is the week's longest ride:** at most 40 % of the week's riding (50 % with three rides, 60 % with two), and at most 6 hours. Sample plans put a 3–5 h long ride in an 8–12 h week, 35–50 % of it.
6. **Every fourth week is lighter, about 70 % of the usual week.** Masters riders often do better with a lighter week every third week ([TrainingPeaks, Friel's over-50 plan](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/training-plans/cycling/road-cycling/tp-2590/bike-road-racing-base-plan-over-50-8-13hpw-power-meter-12-weeks)); VO2 keeps four for everyone for now.
7. **Taper before a main goal ride:** two weeks before an event of five hours or more (85 % then 60 % of the usual week, 50 % in race week), one week before shorter ones (75 %, then 50 %). Cut volume, keep intensity and frequency (Bosquet 2007 meta-analysis: two weeks, volume cut 41–60 %, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17762369/)).
8. **A missed session is skipped.** Never add its time to another ride or add a ride to make it up.

Ride steps are written by time only, never by distance.

### Week structure

Templates by rides per week (guidance):

| Rides | Week                                                                       |
| ----- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 2     | 1 quality or long ride, 1 endurance. Maintenance or beginners only.        |
| 3     | 1 quality, 1 endurance, 1 long.                                            |
| 4     | 1–2 quality, 1–2 endurance, 1 long.                                        |
| 5     | 2 quality, 2 endurance, 1 long.                                            |
| 6     | 2 quality (3 only for experienced riders above 10 h), 3 endurance, 1 long. |

- Most riding is endurance (zone 2). About 80 % of time low intensity is the usual description of good amateurs and elites; it describes, it isn't a prescription (Seiler, [Frontiers 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2015.00295/full)).
- Riders on six hours a week or less can use more sweet spot and threshold: there isn't the volume for a polarized week to work ([CTS, time-crunched](https://trainright.com/learn/cycling/time-crunched-cyclist/)).
- With two quality rides, make them different: one sweet spot or threshold, one VO2 max or event-specific.
- Progress one thing at a time: more time, or more hard work, not both in the same week.

### Long ride

- Endurance zone by default, with some tempo late in build for long events.
- For a gran fondo, century, or gravel event, the long ride builds toward 70–85 % of the event's expected time three to four weeks out, and stays at 60–70 % two weeks out ([Biketips](https://biketips.com/how-to-prepare-for-a-gran-fondo/), [procyclingcoaching](https://www.procyclingcoaching.com/post/how-many-hours-a-week-train-gran-fondo)). The rules above win: never break one to reach it.
- Riding the event's full distance in training isn't needed.
- Fuelling: say it in the why of every ride over 90 minutes. About 30 g of carbohydrate an hour for 1–2 h, 60 g for 2–2.5 h, up to 90 g beyond, from mixed sugars ([Jeukendrup](https://www.mysportscience.com/post/2015/05/27/recommendations-for-carb-intake-during-exercise)). Long rides are where the athlete rehearses event fuelling.

### Season phases

VO2 tells you each week's phase. Shape the rides to it (guidance):

- **Base:** mostly endurance, tempo, short sweet spot, and sprints for leg speed. At most one quality ride a week. The long ride grows.
- **Build:** sweet spot growing to threshold, then VO2 max intervals; the event's specific work grows (_Sessions_).
- **Peak:** the most event-like work and the longest long rides.
- **Taper:** cut time as the budget says; keep one or two short threshold or VO2 max touches with fewer repeats. Nothing new.
- **Lighter week:** the same kinds of ride, fewer repeats, a shorter long ride; drop a quality ride if the week has two.
- **Recovery** (after the main goal or an all-out ride): endurance and recovery rides only.

### Indoor rides

An hour on a trainer has no coasting: it is denser than an hour outside, often counted as 1.2–1.5 outdoor hours ([Roadman](https://roadmancycling.com/compare/indoor-vs-outdoor-training)). When the athlete can only ride indoors, an endurance ride may be about three quarters of its outdoor length. Intervals stay the same.

### Supporting rides

- **For fun:** an easy ride. It is one of the week's rides, an easy one: write one ride fewer, and keep the week's quality rides. It is not a quality ride for rule 4.
- **Strong and steady (70–80 %):** every other ride that week is easy. VO2 trims race week to 85 % and makes the week after lighter.
- **All out:** VO2 gives it the main event's taper and a recovery week after, but no build of its own.
- **Main goal:** the taper above. VO2 adds the event to its week.

Every race but one ridden for fun counts as a quality ride for rule 4.

### Writing for the athlete

- Each session has a short "why": what it trains, in one sentence, in plain words. Mention fuelling on rides over 90 minutes.
- Target zones (_Zones_); VO2 fills in the athlete's watts or heart rate. Never write a number of watts.
- Masters riders (VO2 doesn't know the age yet): when the athlete says they recover slowly, leave more easy rides between hard ones.

## Zones

A ride step targets a **zone** (or an effort, when the athlete rides by feel). VO2 turns the zone into the athlete's own numbers: watts from their FTP when VO2 has one, otherwise heart rate from their cycling LTHR, otherwise the zone's name and feel. Never write a number of watts or bpm yourself.

The levels are Coggan's, as a share of FTP, with sweet spot between tempo and threshold ([TrainingPeaks power levels](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/learn/articles/power-training-levels/), [TrainerRoad sweet spot](https://www.trainerroad.com/sweet-spot-training)). Heart rate is a share of cycling LTHR: cycling LTHR runs lower than running LTHR, so never borrow one sport's ([Friel](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/learn/articles/joe-friel-s-quick-guide-to-setting-zones/)).

| Zone        | % FTP    | % cycling LTHR | Feel                             | For                             |
| ----------- | -------- | -------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| `recovery`  | < 55 %   | < 68 %         | Barely pedalling                 | Recovery rides, recoveries      |
| `endurance` | 56–75 %  | 69–83 %        | All day, full sentences          | Most riding, long rides         |
| `tempo`     | 76–87 %  | 84–90 %        | Steady pressure, short sentences | Muscular endurance, long events |
| `sweetSpot` | 88–94 %  | 91–95 %        | Comfortably hard, a few words    | Threshold gains at low cost     |
| `threshold` | 95–105 % | 95–102 %       | Hard, about an hour's effort     | FTP, time trials, long climbs   |
| `vo2max`    | 106–120% | —              | Very hard, 3–8 minutes           | VO2 max                         |
| `anaerobic` | 121–150% | —              | Near all-out, 30 s to 3 min      | Crits, short climbs, surges     |
| `sprint`    | Max      | —              | All-out, under 30 s              | Neuromuscular power, sprints    |

- Heart rate lags behind short efforts and drifts on long ones. Above threshold it can't steer an effort: VO2 shows power, or the feel.
- Efforts (`easy`, `steady`, `comfortablyHard`, `hard`, `fastRelaxed`) map to endurance, tempo, threshold, VO2 max, and anaerobic. Use an effort instead of a zone when the athlete has neither power nor heart rate, or on rough ground where power swings.
- Work above threshold rarely exceeds 30–40 minutes in one session (TrainingPeaks).

### Where FTP comes from

VO2 estimates FTP from the athlete's rides with power. A tested or race value is better; the athlete's best 20 minutes × 0.95 is the usual estimate ([CTS](https://trainright.com/ftp-tests-how-to-perform-20-minute-8-minute-and-ramp-tests/)). Without power, the heart-rate zones above carry the plan.

## Sessions

The rides VO2 writes, their step format, and how to size them. Every step is a time (`durationS`) and a zone or an effort. A block repeats its steps: 3 × (12 min threshold, 5 min recovery) is one block with repeat 3 and two steps. Warm-up and cool-down are their own blocks. Size every ride from the week's budget and the rules in _Load and weekly structure_; when an example is bigger than the budget allows, take repeats off.

### Kinds

| Kind        | Name              | Structure                                                                                                                  | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------- | ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `recovery`  | Recovery ride     | 30–60 min `recovery`                                                                                                       | After a hard day or an event                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `easy`      | Endurance ride    | 1–3 h `endurance`                                                                                                          | The bulk of the week                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `long`      | Long ride         | 2–6 h `endurance`, some `tempo` late in build for long events                                                              | Fuelling rehearsal                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `sweetSpot` | Sweet spot        | 15 min warm-up; 3–4 × 10–15 min `sweetSpot`, 5 min `recovery`; building to 2 × 20                                          | 60–90 min ([TrainerRoad](https://www.trainerroad.com/sweet-spot-training))                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `tempo`     | Threshold         | 15 min warm-up; 3 × 10–12 min or 2 × 15–20 min `threshold`, recovery about a quarter of the work                           | 20–40 min of work                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `intervals` | VO2 max intervals | 15 min warm-up; 5 × 4 min or 4 × 8 min `vo2max`, recovery about equal to the work (2 min after 8)                          | 12–32 min of work; 4 × 8 did best in Seiler's 2013 comparison                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| `intervals` | Short intervals   | 3 sets of 13 × (30 s `vo2max` to `anaerobic`, 15 s `recovery`), 3 min between sets                                         | Raised VO2 max more than 4 × 5 min ([Rønnestad 2015](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Short%20intervals%20induce%20superior%20training%20adaptations%20compared%20with%20long%20intervals%20in%20cyclists%20%E2%80%93%20An%20effort-matched%20approach.pdf)) |
| `hills`     | Climbing          | Repeats on a climb at the event's climb length: 3–5 × 8–20 min `threshold` or `sweetSpot`; some at low cadence (50–65 rpm) | Low cadence strains knees; a tool, not race cadence                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `sprints`   | Sprints           | Endurance ride with 6–10 × 10–15 s `sprint`, full recovery (3–5 min)                                                       | Leg speed in base; crits                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |

Over-unders (8–12 min alternating 2 min at the top of `threshold` and 1 min `vo2max`) are written as `tempo`. Anaerobic work, 2 × (3 × 1 min `anaerobic`, 1 min easy), as `intervals`.

### By event

Match the work to the event the season builds toward (guidance; [Roadman](https://roadmancycling.com/answers/training-for-a-hill-climb), [FasCat](https://www.fascatcoaching.com/tips/race-specific-cycling-intervals/), [TrainingPeaks](https://trainingpeaks.com/blog/youre-training-too-hard-for-criteriums-heres-why)):

- **Gran fondo or century, flat or rolling:** long rides, tempo and sweet spot; threshold in build.
- **Hilly or mountain gran fondo** (about 15 m of climbing per km or more): climbing repeats as long as the event's climbs, sweet spot and threshold at climb length, back-to-back long weekends when the long ride can't grow further.
- **Gravel:** long endurance base, 3–8 min threshold hill repeats, surges over short pitches; a long event favours steady hours, a short one repeatable hard efforts.
- **Road race or crit:** a solid threshold, then over-unders, 1-minute efforts, and sprints. Too much high intensity erodes endurance.
- **Time trial:** long threshold, building to 2 × 20 min, ideally in the TT position. Intervals about half the race time.
- **Mountain bike (XC):** short climbs and surges, 20–90 s at `anaerobic`, on top of threshold.
- **Hill climb:** threshold or VO2 max efforts at the climb's length.

### Sizing by level

- Under 5 h a week: one quality ride, short (2 × 10 min sweet spot, 5 × 3 min VO2 max).
- 5–8 h: one to two quality rides of 60–90 min.
- 8–12 h: two quality rides; the long ride 3–4 h.
- Over 12 h: two, sometimes three quality rides; the long ride up to 5–6 h.

Fewer repeats in a lighter or taper week, never a new kind of session.

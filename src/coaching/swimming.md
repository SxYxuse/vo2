# Swimming

Everything VO2 knows about planning swims: how a week is built, the zones swims target, and the sessions to write.

## Load and weekly structure

You write the athlete's swims for the coach plan: each week's swim sessions, with no day, inside a weekly swimming budget VO2 sets in metres. The athlete picks the days to swim them. VO2 checks every plan in code. A plan that breaks a rule comes back to you with the reason. Write sessions that pass the first time.

Evidence tags: **rule** means VO2 checks it in code. **Guidance** means coaching convention or weak evidence; follow it unless the athlete's situation gives a reason not to. Sources are linked where a number comes from one.

### Rules VO2 checks

1. **Weekly swimming distance stays inside the budget.** Swims are budgeted in metres: pace in the water barely changes with conditions in a pool, and swim coaches and plans count metres per week. The budget follows the season phase and never exceeds 10 % over the biggest of the last four weeks, or 500 m over it for a small week. After a break, the floor is 60 % of the usual week before it; with no history, swims per week × 1500 m. Plan 95–100 % of the budget. Rest at the wall adds time, not metres.
2. **No swim is more than 15 % longer than the longest swim of the last 30 days**, or 2000 m when there is none. Grow the long swim in steps.
3. **Sessions have no day.** A week holds no more swims than the athlete's swims per week (0–6), fewer in a week already under way. Never name a day.
4. **Quality swims per week:** 1 for 2–3 swims, 2 for 4–5, at most 3 for 6. Across runs, rides and swims together, at most 3 quality sessions a week. Races count. Quality means threshold (CSS), VO2 max sets, sprints, and races; technique and open water swims are not quality.
5. **The long swim is the week's longest swim:** at most 40 % of the week's swimming (50 % with three swims, 60 % with two), and at most 2 hours. Published plans put the longest swim at about 40 % of the week (a 3.8 km swim plan: 4000 m in about 10,600 m a week; a first 5 km open water plan: 4200 m in about 10,700 m).
6. **Every fourth week is lighter, about 70 % of the usual week.** No trial sets the rhythm (guidance in origin); VO2 enforces it because athletes skip it otherwise.
7. **Taper before a main goal swim:** two weeks, 70 % of the usual week, then 50 % in race week. Cut volume, keep intensity and the number of swims (Bosquet 2007 meta-analysis, which leans on swimming studies: about two weeks, volume cut 41–60 %, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17762369/); Mujika and Padilla: keep intensity, cut frequency by no more than 20 %, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12840640/)).
8. **A missed session is skipped.** Never add its metres to another swim or add a swim to make it up.

Every swim step is a distance in metres, except rest at the wall (_Sessions_).

### Week structure

Templates by swims per week (guidance):

| Swims | Week                                                                           |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 2     | 1 threshold (CSS), 1 endurance or long. Maintenance or beginners only.         |
| 3     | 1 quality, 1 technique or endurance, 1 long.                                   |
| 4     | 1–2 quality, 1 technique, 1 endurance, 1 long.                                 |
| 5     | 2 quality, 1 technique, 1 endurance, 1 long.                                   |
| 6     | 2 quality (3 only for experienced swimmers), 1 technique, 2 endurance, 1 long. |

- Most triathletes and fitness swimmers swim two or three times a week; at least two keeps the feel for the water ([TrainingPeaks](https://trainingpeaks.com/blog/balancing-swim-bike-run-in-triathlon-training)).
- Technique pays more than fitness for most adult swimmers: put drills in the warm-up of most swims, and give beginners a technique swim every week.
- With two quality swims, make them different: one threshold, one VO2 max or sprints.
- Progress one thing at a time: more metres, or more hard work, not both in the same week.

### Long swim

- Endurance zone, as one continuous swim or long repeats (400–1000 m) with short rest. Late in build for a long event, some tempo.
- For a 3.8 km or longer goal, the long swim reaches about the event distance two to four weeks out. For 1.9 km and shorter, the event distance is reached early; grow the CSS work instead. Rules 2 and 5 win: never break one to reach it.
- Fuelling: swims over 90 minutes are the place to rehearse feeds, as the event will have them (a 5 km or longer open water swim). Say it in the why.

### Season phases

VO2 tells you each week's phase. Shape the swims to it (guidance):

- **Base:** technique, endurance, short CSS sets, a few sprints for speed. At most one quality swim a week. The long swim grows.
- **Build:** CSS sets grow (more repeats, shorter rest), then VO2 max sets; open water skills for an open water event.
- **Peak:** the most event-like work: long CSS sets, the longest long swims, open water at race effort.
- **Taper:** cut metres as the budget says; keep the number of swims and one short CSS or VO2 max touch with fewer repeats, except in race week, where the race fills the quality sessions. Nothing new.
- **Lighter week:** the same kinds of swim, fewer repeats, a shorter long swim; drop a quality swim if the week has two.
- **Recovery** (after the main goal or an all-out swim): recovery and technique swims only.

### Open water

- In open water there is no wall: sighting, swimming straight, starts, and drafting are skills to practise. Sight every 6 to 10 strokes in calm water, more often in chop ([Tri247](https://www.tri247.com/triathlon-training/swim/open-water-swimming-tips-for-beginners-triathlon)). Lift the eyes, not the head.
- Drafting just behind another swimmer's feet cuts drag by about a fifth and makes the same speed cheaper (Chatard and Wilson 2003, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12840639/)).
- A wetsuit makes the same pace cheaper and the swimmer faster, mostly through buoyancy and body position (Tomikawa 2008, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17387042/)). The athlete should train in the race wetsuit before race day.
- Write open water swims only when the athlete has open water (they say so, or their history shows it). Otherwise, the pool prepares them; say in a why that sighting can be practised in the pool.

### Shoulders

Swimming carries little impact, so an easy swim fits beside hard runs and rides, and triathletes often use it to recover ([Bales 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23147088/)). Its overuse injury is the shoulder: in elite swimmers, supraspinatus tendinopathy rose with hours and metres per week (Sein 2010, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18463295/)). When the athlete reports shoulder pain, keep the budget flat, cut sprints and VO2 max sets, and suggest they see a physio.

### Supporting swims

- **For fun:** an easy swim. It is one of the week's swims: write one swim fewer, and keep the week's quality swims. It is not a quality swim for rule 4.
- **Strong and steady (70–80 %):** every other swim that week is easy. VO2 trims race week to 85 % and makes the week after lighter.
- **All out:** VO2 gives it the main event's taper and a recovery week after, but no build of its own.
- **Main goal:** the taper above. VO2 adds the event to its week.

Every race but one swum for fun counts as a quality swim for rule 4.

### Writing for the athlete

- Each session has a short "why": what it trains, in one sentence of at most 300 characters, in plain words. Drills are steps (_Sessions_), so the athlete sees which drill to swim and when; the title may still name the focus ("Technique: catch-up and 6-kick switch").
- Target zones (_Zones_); VO2 fills in the athlete's pace per 100 m. Never write a pace or a time.

## Zones

A swim step targets a **zone** (or an effort, when VO2 has no CSS). VO2 turns the zone into the athlete's pace per 100 m from their **CSS** (critical swim speed). Never write a pace yourself.

CSS is the speed a swimmer can in theory hold without tiring; it tracks 400 m speed and lactate threshold closely (Wakayoshi 1992, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1521952/)). It sits a little above the true maximal lactate steady state (Dekerle 2005, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16195984/)), so long sets at CSS are hard and need short rests. In practice it is about the pace of a hard 1500 m. The zones are offsets in seconds per 100 m (+ is slower), from Swim Smooth's CSS training ([Swim Smooth](https://blog.swimsmooth.com/p/understanding-training-zones)):

| Zone        | Offset from CSS (s/100 m) | Feel                             | For                             |
| ----------- | ------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| `recovery`  | +11 to +20                | Loose, long strokes              | Recovery swims, drills, warm-up |
| `endurance` | +6 to +10                 | Steady, could keep going         | Most swimming, long swims       |
| `tempo`     | +3 to +5                  | Strong, controlled breathing     | Long repeats, long-event pace   |
| `threshold` | −1 to +2                  | Hard, about a 1500 m race effort | CSS sets                        |
| `vo2max`    | −2 to −6                  | Very hard, about 400 m race pace | VO2 max sets                    |
| `sprint`    | Faster than −6            | All-out                          | 25–50 m sprints, starts         |

- Efforts (`easy`, `steady`, `comfortablyHard`, `hard`, `fastRelaxed`) map to endurance, tempo, threshold, VO2 max, and sprint. Drill steps are always effort `easy` or zone `recovery`.
- Heart rate is hard to read in the water and runs lower than on land; VO2 doesn't steer swims by it.

### Where CSS comes from

VO2 takes the athlete's best 400 m and best 200 m from swims of the last six months: CSS pace per 100 m = (t400 − t200) / 2 ([Swim Smooth's CSS test](https://swimsmoothtips.blogspot.com/2014/04/css-training-for-absolute-beginners.html): a hard 400 m, 5–10 minutes easy, a hard 200 m; retest every 4–6 weeks). Without both, it estimates CSS from the best continuous swim of 1000 m or more, which is easier to get but less exact. Without either, swims go by effort.

## Sessions

The swims VO2 writes, their step format, and how to size them. Every step is a distance (`distanceM`) and a zone or an effort. The one exception is **rest at the wall**: a step with part `recovery`, a time (`durationS`, the seconds of rest) and effort `easy`; it counts toward the session's time but not its metres. A **drill** is a step that also names its drill in `drill`, at effort `easy` or zone `recovery`; every other step has `drill` null. A block repeats its steps: 10 × (100 m threshold, 15 s rest) is one block with repeat 10 and two steps; 6 × (50 m catch-up drill, 15 s rest) is one block with repeat 6. Warm-up and cool-down are their own blocks: typically 200–600 m warm-up (easy, some drills) and 100–300 m cool-down. Size every swim from the week's budget and the rules in _Load and weekly structure_; when an example is bigger than the budget allows, take repeats off.

### Kinds

| Kind        | Name            | Structure                                                                                               | Notes                                                                     |
| ----------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `recovery`  | Recovery swim   | 1000–2000 m `recovery`, easy repeats of 100–200 m with 20 s rest                                        | After a hard day or an event                                              |
| `easy`      | Endurance swim  | 1500–3500 m: 4–10 × 300–400 m `endurance`, 20–30 s rest                                                 | The bulk of the week. Swim Smooth: 10 × 400 m at CSS +6, 30 s rest        |
| `long`      | Long swim       | 2000–5000 m `endurance`, continuous or 400–1000 m repeats with 20–30 s rest; some `tempo` late in build | Event endurance; feeds over 90 minutes                                    |
| `technique` | Technique       | Warm-up; 8–12 × 25–50 m drill steps, `easy`, 15–20 s rest; 4–6 × 100 m `easy` swim using the drill      | Not quality. One or two drills a session; the title names the focus       |
| `tempo`     | Threshold (CSS) | Warm-up; 8–20 × 100 m `threshold`, 10–15 s rest; or 4–6 × 200–400 m, 20–30 s rest                       | 1000–2000 m of work. Hold the pace; slow a little rather than rest longer |
| `intervals` | VO2 max set     | Warm-up; 8–16 × 50 m or 6–10 × 100 m `vo2max`, rest about equal to the work (20–60 s)                   | 400–1200 m of work                                                        |
| `sprints`   | Sprints         | Endurance swim with 8–12 × 25 m `sprint`, 30–60 s rest                                                  | Speed, starts; base and short events                                      |
| `openWater` | Open water      | 1000–4000 m continuous, `endurance` with 2–6 surges of 100–200 m `tempo` or `threshold`                 | Not quality. Sighting, straight lines, wetsuit; no wall, so no rest steps |
| `race`      | Race            | The event distance at race effort                                                                       | VO2 adds it                                                               |

Swim Smooth's "Goldilocks" set (2 × 100, 200; 2 × 100, 300; 2 × 100, 400 at CSS, 20 s rest) is written as `tempo`, one block per group. The drills a step can name, with what each trains (VO2 shows the athlete how to swim each one):

| `drill`         | Drill             | Trains                         |
| --------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------ |
| `catchUp`       | Catch-up          | A long body line, full stroke  |
| `singleArm`     | Single arm        | The catch and rotation         |
| `sixKickSwitch` | 6-kick switch     | Rotation and balance           |
| `fist`          | Fist              | An early, high-elbow catch     |
| `sculling`      | Sculling          | Feel for the water             |
| `kickOnSide`    | Kick on the side  | Balance and body position      |
| `kick`          | Kick with a board | The legs, a high body position |
| `pull`          | Pull with a buoy  | The pull                       |

Drills also fit the warm-up of other swims: 4 × 25–50 m of one drill before the main set.

### By event

Match the work to the event the season builds toward (guidance):

- **Pool 400–1500 m, sprint or Olympic triathlon (750–1500 m):** CSS sets and VO2 max sets carry the build; sprints for starts. A triathlon starts fast: some sets open with 50–100 m at `vo2max` before settling to `threshold`.
- **Half-distance triathlon (1.9 km):** CSS sets with longer repeats (200–400 m), `tempo` repeats, long swim to about 2500–3000 m.
- **Ironman (3.8 km):** long repeats at `tempo` and `endurance`, a long swim reaching about 3500–4000 m, continuous swims in the wetsuit.
- **Open water 5 km and longer:** the most metres; long swims reaching 70–100 % of the event within the 2-hour cap, `tempo` repeats of 400–1000 m, feeding practice. Open water swims when the athlete has access.

### Sizing by level

- Under 4000 m a week: one quality swim, short (8 × 100 m CSS, 8 × 50 m VO2 max); technique in every swim. A technique swim with 50 m drills is about 800 m; in a small week (under about 1000 m a swim) swim the drills as 25 m repeats, 8 × 25 m in a 500–700 m swim.
- 4000–8000 m: one to two quality swims; the long swim 2000–3000 m.
- 8000–12,000 m: two quality swims; the long swim 3000–4000 m.
- Over 12,000 m: two, sometimes three quality swims; the long swim up to 5000 m or the 2-hour cap.

Fewer repeats in a lighter or taper week, never a new kind of session.
